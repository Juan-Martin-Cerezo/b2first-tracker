01The self-study trap

Why studying alone stalls before exam day

Most candidates lose time between two options that both miss what the B2 First marks actually measure.

Traditional language school

Fixed group pace

  • Classes move through textbook units even when you already pass Reading.
  • Writing feedback arrives days later, with little time spent on individual error patterns.
  • Class calendars rarely match your own exam date or weekly hours.
Studying blind alone

Guessing without a rubric

  • Answer keys score multiple-choice gaps, while Writing and Speaking receive zero band feedback.
  • Without an error log, you cannot tell vocabulary gaps apart from time-limit mistakes.
  • Each study night starts with deciding what to open instead of working on a weak part.
What is missing is exam criteria, not more hours.

B2 First Tracker maps your starting level in 12 questions, schedules one timed task per session around your weak parts, and applies the published Cambridge bands to your Writing and Speaking practice.

02The product

Nine modules around one log

Every part of the tracker reads the same practice history, so your daily session, error breakdown, and stage progress agree.

01 · Diagnostic

12-item entry diagnostic

Ten-minute check across Reading and Use of English parts 1 to 7. Identifies your lowest-scoring parts before your first week starts.

02 · Daily focus

Today's session

Shows one prioritized task for your scheduled study day, with its unit timer, target threshold, and the reason it was chosen.

03 · Schedule

Adaptive weekly plan

Choose by-part rotation or full-paper rotation across your weekly study days. Queue order comes from what you have already logged.

04 · Exam mode

Official paper timings

Run timed full-paper sittings with the real durations: 75 min Reading and Use of English, 80 min Writing, 40 min Listening, and 14 min Speaking.

05 · Writing

Cambridge rubric corrector

Scores essays, articles, reports, reviews, and emails (140 to 190 words) on all four 0 to 5 bands with quoted evidence and rewrites.

06 · Speaking

4-part voice examiner

Practise Parts 1 to 4 with timed turns (2, 4, 4, and 4 minutes), a partner voice in Part 3, a full transcript, and a banded report.

07 · Errors

Root-cause taxonomy

Log misses as L-GAP (vocabulary), G-ERR (grammar), T-MIS (distractor), or T-LIM (time limit) with concrete fixes.

08 · Progress

Charts and stage gates

Four charts track score history, part accuracy, error mix, and weekly consistency alongside three deterministic progression stages.

09 · Library & sync

17 parts and 26 sources

Guides for all 17 exam parts, 26 vetted practice links, optional local PDF and audio files, and multi-device account sync.

03Writing & Speaking

Writing scored on the four Cambridge bands

Each draft is checked against the 0 to 5 scale from the B2 First Handbook, within the 140 to 190 word exam target.

You can paste the exam prompt so Content is checked against every point the question asks for. Every band cites quotes from your draft, names two weak spots, and provides three rewritten sentences you can study.

  • Target 140–190 words
  • 4 bands, 0–5 each
  • Quoted draft evidence
  • 3 sentence rewrites

Four-part Speaking with a voice examiner

Run one part at a time or the full 14-minute sequence across Parts 1 to 4. You hear the examiner, answer out loud within each turn timer, and get a transcript and banded report at the end.

It is practice, not a certificate — the real examiner is still a person.

  • Parts 1 to 4 with real exam timings (2, 4, 4, and 4 min)
  • A partner role for Parts 2, 3, and 4
  • Transcript and banded assessment across Cambridge criteria
Speaking timeline 14 min total
  1. Part 12 min · Interview
  2. Part 24 min · Long turn
  3. Part 34 min · Collaborative
  4. Part 44 min · Discussion

Honesty rule: Pronunciation

If audio cannot be measured or speech assessment is not configured on the server, the report explicitly displays not measured for Pronunciation. It never invents a score from text alone.

04Engineering & privacy

Built for study, without ad trackers

Your progress syncs across devices while keeping data collection minimal and reversible.

Multi-device account sync

Sign in with a username and 12-character password, Google sign-in, or a passkey. Your weekly plan, study log, and error notes follow you from phone to desktop.

No advertising trackers

The app runs without third-party analytics SDKs, tracking pixels, or ad networks. Nothing you practise is profiled or sold.

Audio is never stored

Speaking audio is never written to the provider's disk. Only the writing draft or transcript you choose to evaluate is sent to the AI provider per request.

Export and permanent deletion

Your data lives on the provider's server while your account is active. You can export everything as JSON or delete your account to remove your data from the server.

05FAQ

Four direct answers

Short answers on the diagnostic, accounts, privacy, and scoring.

How does the 12-item diagnostic shape my plan?

The diagnostic checks Reading and Use of English parts 1 to 7 in about 10 minutes. Your lowest-scoring parts move to the front of your weekly plan and appear in Today's session.

Do I need an email address to sign up?

No. You can create an account with a username and a password of at least 12 characters, or sign in with Google or a passkey.

What happens if I delete my account?

Deleting your account from the Account panel removes your user record, sessions, progress history, and error log from the provider's server. Export a JSON copy first if you want to keep your log.

Are Writing and Speaking bands official Cambridge scores?

No. They are practice estimates calibrated against the four published Cambridge scales. Only Cambridge Assessment issues official exam grades.
